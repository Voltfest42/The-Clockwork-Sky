# Data files

Compact data files used by the course, built by the scripts in `scripts/` from raw downloads. Raw downloads go to `scripts/.cache/` and are not committed. Every file here is listed below with its source and licence, and credited on the site's About page.

Files under a share-alike licence (such as the HYG star catalogue, CC BY-SA 4.0) are kept separate from the rest and marked as such.

The test fixture `tests/fixtures/horizons.json` (geocentric astrometric positions of the Sun, the Moon and the planets at 42 dates from 3000 BC to AD 3000) comes from NASA JPL's Horizons system (DE441), a public service, and is fetched by `scripts/fetch-horizons.ts`.

The test fixture `tests/fixtures/galilean.json` (the positions of Jupiter's four large moons every 10 days for 60 days from 1 January 2026) comes from JPL Horizons (JUP365) and is fetched by `scripts/fetch-galilean.ts`.

The test fixture `tests/fixtures/orrery.json` (the same bodies' positions a Julian century after `orrery.json`'s, on 2 January 2126) comes from JPL Horizons (DE441) and is fetched by `scripts/fetch-orrery.ts`.

The test fixture `tests/fixtures/eclipses.json` (every solar and lunar eclipse from 1900 to 2100: date and time of greatest eclipse, Saros series, type, gamma and magnitudes) comes from NASA's *Five Millennium Catalogs of Solar and Lunar Eclipses* by Fred Espenak, and is fetched by `scripts/fetch-eclipses.ts`. NASA allows the data to be reproduced with the acknowledgment **"Eclipse Predictions by Fred Espenak (NASA's GSFC)"**, which the file carries. The site itself doesn't ship it: its eclipses come from the course's own eclipse finder, which the fixture tests.

## Part II's data files

- `src/data/gaia/hr.json` (**CC BY-NC 3.0 IGO**, in its own folder with a README): the HR diagram of Gaia DR3's stars within 100 parsecs, built by `scripts/build-hr.ts` from the Gaia archive.
- `src/data/xsl/spectra.json` (**CC BY 4.0**, in its own folder with a README): ten stellar spectra from the X-shooter Spectral Library DR2 (Gonneau et al. 2020), from the ESO Science Archive, built by `scripts/build-spectra.ts`.
- `src/data/spectra/lines.json`: 49 spectral lines (air wavelengths, lower-level energies and log gf from the transition probabilities) from the NIST Atomic Spectra Database, fetched 29 Oct 2026 by `scripts/fetch-lines.ts`. Facts, cited.
- `src/data/dwarfSequence.json`: Mamajek's mean dwarf colours, temperatures, bolometric corrections and absolute magnitudes (version 2022.04.16), built by `scripts/build-dwarfs.ts`. No licence stated; cite Pecaut & Mamajek (2013, *ApJS* 208, 9), as the author asks.
- `src/data/spectra/lamps.ts`: the brightest visible lines of hydrogen, helium, sodium, neon and mercury lamps (NIST strong lines; brightnesses schematic).
- `src/data/sunComposition.ts`: the Sun's photospheric abundances of fifteen elements (Asplund, Amarsi & Grevesse 2021, *A&A* 653, A141).
- `src/data/debcat.json`: the masses, radii, surface gravities, temperatures and luminosities (with uncertainties, as log₁₀) of the 195 detached eclipsing binaries in John Southworth's DEBCat, in the copy kept by VizieR as V/152 (version of 10 October 2017, through the Harvard mirror), built by `scripts/build-debcat.ts`. Free to use with a citation of Southworth (2015, ASP Conference Series 496, 164). About 13 kB gzipped.
- `src/data/solarModel.json`: the Sun's insides from Model S (Christensen-Dalsgaard et al. 1996, *Science* 272, 1286; `fgong.l5bi.d.15` from users-phys.au.dk/jcd/solar_models): temperature, density, pressure, hydrogen fraction, and the mass and power inside, at 117 radii, in SI units, with the base of the convection zone and the radius inside which 99% of the power is made, built by `scripts/build-solar-model.ts`. Published for anyone to use with that citation. About 3 kB gzipped.
- `src/data/silso/` (**CC BY-NC 4.0** for SILSO's file, in its own folder with a README): the monthly sunspot numbers since 1749 and every cycle's minimum and maximum (WDC-SILSO, Royal Observatory of Belgium), and the butterfly diagram since 1874 (Royal Greenwich Observatory and USAF/NOAA records compiled by D. Hathaway), built by `scripts/build-sunspots.ts`. About 38 kB gzipped together.
- `src/data/gaia/clusters.json` (**CC BY-NC 3.0 IGO**, in the Gaia folder with its README): the members of six star clusters from Gaia DR3, chosen by position, motion and parallax, built by `scripts/build-clusters.ts`. About 27 kB gzipped.
- `src/data/cepheids.ts`: Leavitt's 25 Cepheids in the Small Magellanic Cloud (Harvard College Observatory Circular 173, 1912, read from the scan in NASA's ADS; public domain); δ Cephei and ℓ Carinae with their parallax distances (Benedict et al. 2002 and 2007) and Benedict et al.'s (2007) period–luminosity law.
- `src/data/records.ts` (also Chapter 16): the heaviest neutron star measured well (PSR J0952−0607, Romani et al. 2022) and the heaviest black hole known from a star in the Galaxy (Gaia BH3, Gaia Collaboration 2024), registered in `asOf.ts`.
- `src/data/exoplanets.json`: every confirmed exoplanet in the NASA Exoplanet Archive's Planetary Systems Composite Parameters table (6,451 on 31 Oct 2026): name, host, method, year and facility of discovery, period, orbit, measured mass (or least mass) and measured radius, and each host's temperature, luminosity and distance, built by `scripts/fetch-exoplanets.ts` through the Archive's TAP service. Masses and radii the Archive works out from each other are left out. The Archive asks for an acknowledgement and a citation of Christiansen et al. (2025, *PSJ*), given on the About page; it states no other terms. About 93 kB gzipped, loaded only with Chapter 19. Registered in `asOf.ts` (every 6 months), with its counts in `src/data/exoplanets.ts`, which also holds PLATO's launch date and K2-18 b's disputed gases (**as of Oct 2026**).
## Part III's data files

- `src/data/nuclides.json`: the ground states of the 3,361 known nuclides (244 stable): protons, neutrons, atomic mass excess (and whether it is measured or extrapolated from systematics), half-life, the two main decay modes with their branches, natural abundance, spin and parity, and year of discovery, from the IAEA's LiveChart of Nuclides API (`nds.iaea.org/relnsd/v1/data?fields=ground_states&nuclides=all`; ENSDF half-lives and decays with NUBASE2020 and AME2020 masses; extracted by the IAEA on 18 Oct 2023, fetched 5 Nov 2026), built by `scripts/build-nuclides.ts`. The IAEA's terms allow adapting and copying for teaching with an acknowledgement, given on the About page. About 40 kB gzipped, loaded only with Chapter 23.
- `src/data/kadonis.json`: the Maxwellian-averaged (n,γ) cross sections at kT = 30 keV of the 357 isotopes in KADoNiS v0.3 (Dillmann et al.; `exp-astro.de/kadonis1.0`, "all recommended MACS"), built by `scripts/build-kadonis.ts`. Cited as the database asks; no other terms stated.
- `src/data/abundances.json`: the Sun's photospheric and CI-chondrite abundances of 83 elements (Asplund, Amarsi & Grevesse 2021, *A&A* 653, A141, Table 2; open access, CC BY 4.0), and the Solar System's abundances of 174 isotopes from gallium to uranium (Lodders et al. 2009, per 10⁶ Si atoms) with their s- and r-process shares, and 53 elements' s, r and p shares (Prantzos, Abia, Cristallo, Limongi & Chieffi 2020, *MNRAS* 491, 1832), read from the papers' arXiv sources (2105.01661, 1911.02545) by `scripts/build-abundances.ts`. Facts, cited.
- `src/data/origins/origins.json` (**CC BY-SA 4.0**, in its own folder with a README): each element's shares by origin from Jennifer Johnson's periodic table (2017; *Science* 363, 474, 2019), as read off her figure by C. M. G. Lee for Wikimedia Commons, built by `scripts/build-origins.ts`.
- `src/data/whiteDwarfs.ts`: the masses and radii of Sirius B, Procyon B, 40 Eridani B and ZTF J1901+1458 with their errors, from their papers (Bond et al. 2015, 2017; Mason et al. 2017; Romero et al. 2019 after Joyce et al. 2018; Caiazzo et al. 2021). Facts, cited; checked 5 Nov 2026.
- `src/data/supernovae.ts`: SN 2011fe's bolometric peak and nickel mass (Pereira et al. 2013) and SN 1987A's (Hamuy et al. 1988 as revised by Taddia et al. 2012; Bouchet et al. 1991), as measured numbers rather than whole light curves, whose terms of use are unclear (Part III plan §13, question 5). Facts, cited.
- `src/data/records.ts` (also Chapter 24): the heaviest white dwarf measured well, ZTF J1901+1458 (Caiazzo et al. 2021), registered in `asOf.ts`.

- `src/core/spectra/saha.ts`: ionisation energies (NIST), partition functions near 6,000 K (Gray 2005, Appendix D) and abundances (Asplund et al. 2021) for the toy atmosphere; a main-sequence electron pressure read from model photospheres.

## Neutron stars and pulsars (Stage 23)

- `eos.ts`: ten of Read, Lackey, Owen & Friedman's (2009) piecewise-polytrope fits to published equations of state (their Table III), with the SLy crust they share (their Table II). Facts from a paper, cited. Transcribed from the paper's HTML on arXiv (0812.2163) on 10 Nov 2026; the TOV solver reproduces every fit's maximum mass and 1.4 M☉ radius as the table gives them (tests/chapter26.test.ts).
- `neutronStars.ts`: the heaviest pulsars' masses (Antoniadis et al. 2013; Fonseca et al. 2021; Romani et al. 2022) and NICER's radii, each team's analysis separately (Riley et al. 2019, 2021; Miller et al. 2019, 2021; Choudhury et al. 2024), and GW170817's radius (LVK 2018). Facts, cited. Dated: `asOf.ts` "nicer".
- `pulsars.ts`: about sixty famous pulsars' periods and spin-down rates, each from the paper that found or timed it (named in each record), with a note on why it is famous.
